Organic Content Plan, H2 2026

Owner: Barbora Stuchlá. Status: Draft for review with Jake. Date: May 28, 2026.

The five content pillars

Every piece of content maps to one of these five pillars. The first four mirror OAK'S LAB's brand pillars. The fifth, AI-Native Delivery, is a content pillar in its own right because AI is too central to OAK'S LAB's positioning right now to sit only as a thread within the others.

Pillar What it covers Primary audience
Product Thinking How OAK'S LAB approaches product decisions: what to build, what to defer, how to prioritize. Discovery, validation, roadmap discipline. CPOs and CTOs
Deep Domain Expertise Vertical-specific engineering and product knowledge. Finance, Healthcare, Cybersecurity, Education. Compliance-by-design, regulatory architecture, vertical patterns. CTOs and engineering leads in priority verticals
Elite Engineering Talent The OAK'S LAB Way: how the team hires, how they ramp, how they deliver. Methodology, code quality, pairing, the "no ramp-up theater" claim made concrete. CTOs and engineering leads
Long-Term Partnership What 4-year partnerships look like in practice. Embed vs Autonomous. Build vs partner economics. The handover model. CTOs, CPOs, and founders
AI-Native Delivery Both halves of AI in OAK'S LAB. AI in how we work (3× dev velocity, AI-augmented engineering) and AI in what we build (LLMs, RAG, agentic systems). CTOs, CPOs, engineering and product leads

Industry reports (3 in H2)

Three flagship gated PDFs. Each unlocks four to six weeks of distribution derivatives.

R1. AI in Production: A 2026 Playbook for Product Teams

August. Pillar: AI-Native Delivery.

Who it's for. CTOs and CPOs at high-growth SaaS and regulated-industry companies who have AI on the roadmap but haven't shipped a production-grade AI feature yet. The "everyone has AI on their roadmap, few have shipped it reliably" audience.

Why it exists. Sets the year's authority arc on AI before the vertical reports land in Q4. Establishes OAK'S LAB as a credible voice on AI in production. Not the "play with LLMs" crowd, the "ship it with monitoring, evals, and reliability" crowd. Also the lowest-risk first report (framework-heavy, light on named-client dependencies).

What it is. A 20-page designed PDF plus a companion AI Delivery Maturity Model framework that distributes independently. Aggregated cross-client patterns rather than original research.

Cluster pair. Ships paired with the new AI Native Development services page. The page is the always-public summary, the report is the gated deep dive.

Build window. Kickoff mid-June. Draft mid-July. Design and review early August. Launch second week of August.

R2. Engineering in Healthcare: A Practitioner's Playbook for 2026

October. Working title. Pillar: Deep Domain Expertise.

Who it's for. Engineering leads and senior engineers at high-growth healthcare companies. Not just CTOs. People building telehealth, EHR-integrated, or clinical-workflow products who hit HIPAA, FHIR, and adoption walls. Practitioner-leaning, not boardroom.

Why it exists. Healthcare is a priority vertical (Track 1) with a strong client story (OpenLoop) but we're under-published in the space. This report establishes domain authority and gives the Healthcare industry page a gated download to point at. Q4 timing because September is too soon after R1, December is owned by R3.

What it is. An 18-page PDF, pattern-library content. Topics include HIPAA-by-architecture, EHR integration patterns, clinical workflow fit, and the adoption-versus-compliance trade-off. Heavy on anonymized examples and external regulatory data.

Named-client risk. OpenLoop permission uncertain. Designed around anonymized vignettes ("a high-growth healthcare client we worked with on telehealth scaling..."). Named-client upgrades drop in at design stage if permissions land.

Build window. Kickoff mid-August (overlaps R1 launch, the first sequencing risk). Draft September. Design first three weeks of October. Launch third week of October.

R3. State of Engineering in Finance 2026

December. Pillar: Deep Domain Expertise.

Who it's for. CTOs at high-growth finance companies. Capital markets, lending, payments, investment banking, private markets. Currently the highest-intent inbound segment.

Why it exists. Three reasons.

  1. Finance is the primary paid angle (Track 1, around 60% of LinkedIn budget). This report proves the claim by showing depth, not asserting it.
  2. Q1 2027 budget cycles. Finance CTOs plan partner spend in December and January, and a flagship report lands in their decision window.
  3. Original research is the biggest investment we can make in domain authority, and December gives July through October to run a CTO survey through outbound.

What it is. A 22-page designed PDF plus an interactive content hub page with chart-by-chart unpacking. Original CTO survey anchors three of six sections (compliance, AI in finance, talent and build-vs-partner). The other three are framework pieces from delivery experience.

Original research. CTO survey, 50+ responses target. Outbound owns outreach (July through October).

Named-client risk. NPM permission uncertain. CMG and Future Mortgage TBD. The spine is survey data, not named-client vignettes, so durability is higher than R2 on this dimension.

Build window. Kickoff late September. Survey live July through October. Analysis October through early November. Draft mid-November. Design early December. Launch second week of December.

Gated downloadables (4)

In addition to the industry reports. Four downloadables for the Convert stage, each targeting a specific buyer persona. Heavy repurpose from existing content. The lift is more synthesis and design than original writing.

D1. SaaS Scaling Guide v4

June. Pillar: Elite Engineering Talent. Status: in design.

CTOs at growth-stage SaaS scaling 20 to 200 engineers. Final approval done. With design now, then ships.

D2. Choosing a Dev Partner: A CTO's Buyer's Guide

Q3 to Q4 (idea). Pillar: Long-Term Partnership.

Who it's for. CTOs in active evaluation of a development partner. Bottom-of-funnel.

Why it exists. Direct sales aid. Gives prospects a structured framework instead of a sales pitch. The "when not to hire us" section earns trust and weeds out poor-fit leads early.

What it is. A 15 to 25 page gated PDF. Sections: decision framework for evaluating partners, structured comparison rubric, "when not to hire OAK" honesty section, engagement model overview (Embed vs Autonomous), red flags to look for in a dev partner pitch.

Source material. Around 60% repurpose from sales one-pagers, voice profiles, and partnership content. Around 40% net new (decision framework, comparison rubric, "when not to hire us" section).

D3. The Roadmap Reality Check: A CPO's Field Guide to What to Build, What to Defer, and What to Kill

Q3 to Q4 (idea). Pillar: Product Thinking.

Who it's for. CPOs at high-growth companies running overloaded roadmaps. The "more product initiatives than your team can handle" audience. Targets product leaders, not engineering.

Why it exists. Everything else in the content engine is oriented toward CTOs. CPOs are equally critical buyers per the ICP but currently have nothing built for them. This downloadable closes that gap.

What it is. An 18 to 25 page gated PDF. Sections: the roadmap audit (identifying the 30% you should cut today), discovery (how much is enough), prioritization frameworks that survive contact with the executive team, when AI features earn the slot, how to say no (to the board, to sales, to engineering), the "what we'd defer if forced" exercise.

Source material. Around 50% repurpose from the OAK'S LAB Way Discovery methodology, the Dual-Track Agile pieces in the Knowledge Hub, and existing voice profile content. Around 50% net new (CPO-facing frameworks, anti-patterns, decision exercises).

D4. 20-to-200 Engineering Scaling Playbook

Q4 (idea). Pillar: Elite Engineering Talent.

Who it's for. CTOs at companies scaling engineering from around 20 to around 200. Post-Series B, pre-IPO, growing fast and feeling the cracks in the org. The exact pressure point OAK'S LAB is best at.

Why it exists. More granular sales tool than the SaaS Scaling Guide. Lets outbound point at a specific, named problem. Generates Convert-stage MQLs because the audience self-selects on a known pain point.

What it is. A 20 to 25 page gated PDF. Pedagogical playbook with frameworks, anti-patterns, anonymized client patterns. Sections: org-shape inflection points, when to hire vs partner, what breaks first as you scale, how to maintain product velocity through team growth.

Source material. Around 50% repurpose from SaaS Scaling Guide structure and anonymized client patterns. Around 50% net new (pedagogical framing, anti-patterns, frameworks).

Decision point. Confirm by end of August which of D2, D3, D4 land in Q4 vs slip to Q1 2027.

T1. The Four-Year Partnership Pattern

Pillar: Long-Term Partnership.

Audience. CTOs and founders at high-growth companies evaluating dev partners.

What it is. What 4-year client retention actually looks like inside OAK'S LAB. The structural reasons: no fixed-fee project pressure, dedicated team continuity, embed model. Counter-story to the "agency churn" pattern most CTOs have seen. References Blackpoint (3 funding rounds), CMG (7+ years), OpenLoop. Frames partnership as a discipline, not a tagline.

T2. Why "More Engineers" Is Almost Never the Answer

Pillar: Product Thinking.

Audience. CTOs and CPOs under pressure to hire fast as delivery slows.

What it is. Argues the default response to slowing delivery (hire more engineers) almost always misses the real bottleneck (product decisions, prioritization, architecture). What CTOs should check first. The economics of hire vs build vs partner. Reframes the question as a product question, not a capacity question.

Role. Establishes Product Thinking as a pillar.

July

T3. Inside the OAK'S LAB Way: How Senior Engineers Ramp in Week One

Pillar: Elite Engineering Talent.

Audience. CTOs evaluating partners, frustrated by ramp-up timelines from previous engagements.

What it is. Methodology piece. What "no ramp-up theater" actually means in practice. The week-one playbook: how OAK engineers absorb a codebase, identify risks, and contribute by Friday. The role of pairing, code review, and the "shadow first, ship second" pattern. Builds credibility for the elite-talent claim by showing how it works.

Role. First Elite Engineering Talent article. Closes the methodology gap.

T4. Production AI Patterns: RAG (Series 1 of 2)

Pillar: AI-Native Delivery.

Audience. Senior engineers and engineering leads at companies building AI features and hitting production walls.

Pillar: AI-Native Delivery.

T8. The Real Cost of a 12-Month Engineering Push: Hire vs Partner

Pillar: Long-Term Partnership.

Audience. CTOs and CFOs running build-vs-partner economics for a 12-month engineering scale-up.

What it is. Full TCO comparison of hiring in-house vs partnering for a 12-month push. Includes the hidden costs of hiring: recruiting time, ramp time, attrition risk, severance exposure if priorities shift. Argues the partnership model wins on velocity AND cost for 12-to-18-month windows.

Email nurture flows (2)

Until now the content engine has treated downloadable submits as fuel for HubSpot lists and ad retargeting. The email inboxes themselves haven't been worked. Email nurture closes that gap. Two flows because website downloads and LinkedIn Lead Gen submits come from very different intent levels.

Operating model. Both flows are short, link-heavy emails (three to five sentences plus a link), not long-form newsletters. The job is to pull the lead back to oakslab.com, where the existing content engine (industry pages, services pages, Situations pages, case studies, articles, KH library, and one-pagers) does the conversion work. Each email points at one specific asset. Built and run as HubSpot workflows.

Flow A: Website downloads (warm, came on purpose)

Trigger. Form submit on any gated downloadable from oakslab.com (D1, D2, D3, D4, plus R1, R2, R3 reports as they ship).

Audience signal. Came to the site on purpose. Filled in a form deliberately. Likely already evaluating partners or product-development approaches.

Cadence. Five emails over around four weeks.

Email Day Content Link out
E1 Immediate Delivery plus a short expanded thought Relevant industry or services page
E2 Day 3 One framing line on a common mistake or pattern TL article or KH piece on the topic
E3 Day 8 "Here's how a client handled this at scale" Industry-swap case study (NPM, Blackpoint, OpenLoop, or Swiftly based on HubSpot industry field; default NPM if unknown)
E4 Day 18 Soft meeting CTA, framed for "before we talk" Industry-specific one-pager plus calendar link
E5 Day 28 Final touch ("the newest thing from us") Most recent industry report (R1, R2, or R3)

Flow B: LinkedIn Lead Gen (cold, scrolling)

Trigger. Form submit on a LinkedIn Lead Gen form attached to a downloadable promotion.

Audience signal. Was scrolling LinkedIn. Submitted because the gate was low-friction. May not remember opting in. Way colder than Flow A.

Cadence. Four emails over around four weeks. Gentler, fewer asks, more "we know you might be cold" framing.

Email Day Content Link out
E1 Immediate Delivery plus a one-line reminder of who OAK'S LAB is Most relevant industry or services page
E2 Day 5 to 7 "Did you get to it? The bit on X usually surprises people." One follow-on TL article or KH piece
E3 Day 14 "If you want the one-page version" Industry-specific one-pager
E4 Day 28 Polite wind-down plus a final meeting CTA Calendar link plus reminder they're on the quarterly newsletter

Tooling. HubSpot workflows. All leads land in HubSpot already. This just attaches a sequence to the lifecycle event.

Sequencing. Flow A goes live first, paired with the SaaS Scaling Guide v4 launch and the R1 AI report launch (August). Flow B goes live alongside the first LinkedIn Lead Gen ad campaign, likely Q4.
