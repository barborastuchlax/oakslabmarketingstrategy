Every piece of content maps to one of these five pillars. AI-Native Delivery leads — AI is now central to how OAK'S LAB delivers and how clients evaluate partners. Outsourcing is the explicit second-tier conversation: owning the term CTOs actually search for, rather than dancing around it. Editorial principle — AI delivery vs AI as product feature

Two halves of AI sit inside OAK'S LAB and content readers conflate them constantly:

  • AI delivery — how OAK'S LAB itself uses AI to build faster and better (AI-augmented engineering, dev tooling, workflow restructuring). Lives in W6 AI Native Development services page, R1 (AI-Native Delivery report), R3 (Tech Stack 2027), and most AI-Native Delivery pillar content.
  • AI as product feature — what clients build into their own products (LLMs, RAG, agentic systems for end users). Lives in W8 AI Agentic Systems services page and client-side AI case studies.

Every AI-related piece must make clear which half it's about — in the title, the first paragraph, and the page or section it lives in. Conflating the two muddies the positioning R1 + W6 establish.

T6. The Product Roadmap Test: 5 Questions Every CPO Should Ask Before Approving the Next Sprint

Product Thinking

Audience CPOs at high-growth companies running overloaded roadmaps. Also product managers and engineering leads who run roadmap reviews.

What it is A discovery + prioritization framework named in five specific questions, designed to be asked aloud at every sprint review:

  1. What user evidence triggered this initiative? (continuous-discovery anchor)
  2. What's the smallest version that tests our riskiest assumption? (scope discipline)
  3. What evidence would change our minds? (falsifiable claim)
  4. What are we explicitly deferring to make room? (opportunity cost)
  5. What would we cut if forced to ship in half the time? (prioritization stress test)

Plus the forced-ranking exercise: rank top 10 initiatives, mark the bottom 3 as paused for 90 days. If you can't bring yourself to mark three, the problem isn't your prioritization framework — it's your product strategy. CPO-led discipline that closes the gap most product orgs only address at re-org moments.

Role First CPO-targeted article. Companion piece to D3 (The Roadmap Reality Check downloadable). Pairs as the public-access summary; D3 is the gated deeper exercise.

T12. Compliance Across Verticals: A CTO's Operating Framework

Elite Engineering Talent (Replaces the original finance-specific T12 — Jake's call. Cross-vertical operating framework, not single-industry deep dive.)

Audience CTOs at high-growth companies operating across multiple verticals — or anticipating expansion into regulated ones — who need a compliance approach that scales by default rather than requiring vertical-specific compliance specialists for each new industry.

What it is An operating framework for compliance that travels across verticals rather than depending on industry-specific specialists. Three pillars:

  1. Team operating practices — background checks, vetting, access controls, separation of duties. The hire-side discipline that makes compliance possible at scale.
  2. Sensitive data handling — defaults that work across HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOC 2, and ISO 27001 contexts. Architectural patterns that don't require rebuilding per vertical: encryption, audit logging, data minimization, environment isolation.
  3. Velocity preservation — how compliance becomes an enabler, not a tax. The processes that let an AI-native delivery team stay fast under compliance constraints rather than slowing to a regulated-industry crawl.

Cross-industry treatment vs deep single-industry approach: where vertical-specific reports (R2 healthcare, future verticals) go deep on one industry's compliance reality, this piece zooms out to the framework that operates across them all.

Role Methodology piece for the Elite Engineering Talent pillar — establishes the "compliant by default, not by specialization" claim that anchors OAK'S LAB's cross-vertical operating story. Complements T10 (healthcare-deep) by providing the framework that operates across verticals.
