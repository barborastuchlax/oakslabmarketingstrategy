The five content pillars

Every piece of content maps to one of these five pillars. The first four mirror OAK'S LAB's brand pillars. The fifth — AI-Native Delivery — is a content pillar in its own right because AI is too central to OAK'S LAB's positioning right now to sit only as a thread within the others.

# Pillar What it covers Primary audience
1 Product Thinking How OAK'S LAB approaches product decisions — what to build, what to defer, how to prioritize. Discovery, validation, roadmap discipline. CPOs and CTOs
2 Deep Domain Expertise Vertical-specific engineering and product knowledge. Finance, Healthcare, Cybersecurity, Education. Compliance-by-design, regulatory architecture, vertical patterns. CTOs and engineering leads in priority verticals
3 Elite Engineering Talent The OAK'S LAB Way — how the team hires, how they ramp, how they deliver. Methodology, code quality, pairing, the "no ramp-up theater" claim made concrete. CTOs and engineering leads
4 Long-Term Partnership What 4-year partnerships look like in practice. Embed vs Autonomous. Build vs partner economics. The handover model. CTOs, CPOs, and founders
5 AI-Native Delivery Both halves of AI in OAK'S LAB — AI in how we work (3× dev velocity, AI-augmented engineering) and AI in what we build (LLMs, RAG, agentic systems). CTOs, CPOs, engineering and product leads

Article-by-article — what each piece is about, who writes it, who it's for

T1 — The Four-Year Partnership Pattern · June · Jake · Long-Term Partnership

Audience: CTOs and founders at high-growth companies evaluating dev partners. What it is: What 4-year client retention actually looks like inside OAK'S LAB. The structural reasons (no fixed-fee project pressure, dedicated team continuity, embed model). Counter-story to the "agency churn" pattern most CTOs have seen. References Blackpoint (3 funding rounds), CMG (7+ years), OpenLoop. Frames partnership as a discipline, not a tagline. Role: Sets tone for the year. Opens the partnership argument that T8 and T11 close.

T2 — Why "More Engineers" Is Almost Never the Answer · June · Jake · Product Thinking

Audience: CTOs and CPOs under pressure to hire fast as delivery slows. What it is: Argues the default response to slowing delivery — "hire more engineers" — almost always misses the real bottleneck (product decisions, prioritization, architecture). What CTOs should check first. The economics of hire vs build vs partner. Reframes the question as a product question, not a capacity question. Role: Establishes Product Thinking as a pillar. Josh amplifies on his channel.

T3 — Inside the OAK'S LAB Way: How Senior Engineers Ramp in Week One · July · Jan Barta (Head of Engineering) · Elite Engineering Talent

Audience: CTOs evaluating partners, frustrated by ramp-up timelines from previous engagements. What it is: Methodology piece. What "no ramp-up theater" actually means in practice. The week-one playbook: how OAK engineers absorb a codebase, identify risks, and contribute by Friday. The role of pairing, code review, and the "shadow first, ship second" pattern. Builds credibility for the elite-talent claim by showing how it works. Role: First Elite Engineering Talent article — Jan's first major byline. Proves a claim that's currently made repeatedly but never demonstrated in content.

T4 — Production AI Patterns: RAG (Series 1 of 3) · July · DMT (Krystof or rotating) · AI-Native Delivery

Audience: Senior engineers and engineering leads at companies building AI features and hitting production walls. What it is: First in the three-part Production AI Patterns series. What we've learned from shipping RAG to production: where they break, what scales, the architectural decisions that matter. Concrete patterns with code-level honesty. Role: Pre-warms the August R1 audience by establishing AI authority in advance. Nur ghostwrites the technical detail.

T5 — Why Founders Get the AI Question Wrong · August · Jake · AI-Native Delivery

Audience: Founders and CTOs treating AI as a feature to add rather than an architecture decision to make. What it is: Companion to R1. The common founder mistake: treating AI as a layered-on capability instead of an architecture. What "AI-native" actually means, and why it changes the build vs buy calculus. Punchier and more opinionated than the report. Role: R1 launch companion. Designed to drive report downloads.

T6 — The Product Roadmap Test: 5 Questions Every CPO Should Ask Before Approving the Next Sprint · August · Matej (Head of Product) · Product Thinking

Audience: CPOs at high-growth companies running overloaded roadmaps. Also product managers, engineering leads who run roadmap reviews. What it is: A discovery-and-prioritization framework. Five questions to ask the roadmap before approving the next sprint. The "what we'd cut if we had to" exercise. Why CPO-led discipline beats CTO-led complaints about scope creep. Role: First CPO-targeted article. Matej's first major byline. Pairs with D2 downloadable to launch Matej as a Product voice.

T7 — Production AI Patterns: Eval Loops (Series 2 of 3) · September · DMT (Krystof) · AI-Native Delivery

Audience: Engineering leads who shipped AI features and are now staring at quality drift, regressions, and "demo works but production doesn't." What it is: Series 2. How to evaluate AI outputs in production. Patterns for eval datasets, golden-set testing, regression detection, human-in-the-loop sampling. Practitioner-honest about the limits of each. Role: Sustains AI authority post-R1 launch.

T8 — The Real Cost of a 12-Month Engineering Push: Hire vs Partner · September · Theo (Executive Director & Co-Founder) · Long-Term Partnership

Audience: CTOs and CFOs running build-vs-partner economics for a 12-month engineering scale-up. What it is: Theo's exec POV piece. Full TCO comparison of hiring in-house vs partnering for a 12-month push. Includes the hidden costs of hiring: recruiting time, ramp time, attrition risk, severance exposure if priorities shift. Argues the partnership model wins on velocity AND cost for 12–18-month windows. Role: Theo's first piece in this cycle — high-credibility exec voice. Jake amplifies for distribution.

T9 — AI Code Review: What's Actually Working in Production · October · DMT (Krystof) · Elite Engineering Talent

Audience: Engineering leads thinking about how to operationalize AI in their own SDLC, not just their products. What it is: Practitioner piece on AI-assisted code review in OAK'S LAB's own delivery process. What's working, what's not, where AI catches bugs humans miss, and where AI generates false confidence. Doubles as proof for the "3× development speed" claim that runs through OAK'S LAB marketing. Role: Second Elite Engineering Talent piece. Cross-threads into AI-Native Delivery — concrete evidence behind the AI-augmented-engineering claim.

T10 — Compliance-by-Design: Avoiding the Late-Stage Rewrite · October · Jake · Deep Domain Expertise (healthcare)

Audience: Engineering leads at healthcare companies. Founders evaluating whether their compliance approach will survive scaling. What it is: R2 companion. The pattern that kills healthcare product launches: building first, layering compliance on at the end. Real cost of late-stage HIPAA rewrites. How OAK'S LAB designs HIPAA into the architecture from commit one. Role: R2 launch companion. Drives Healthcare report downloads.

T11 — Why We Pair Product and Engineering, Not Product Then Engineering · November · Matej (Head of Product) · Long-Term Partnership

Audience: CTOs and CPOs running product orgs where product hands off to engineering in a waterfall pattern. What it is: The OAK'S LAB methodology of running product and engineering in parallel, not sequentially. Why "Product hands off to Engineering" is the source of half the rework in your sprint. Concrete examples of the parallel-pairing model in client work. Role: Matej's second byline — builds his platform. Cross-threads to Product Thinking and Elite Engineering Talent. Pillar tag is Long-Term Partnership because the parallel-pairing model is a core part of how 4-year partnerships hold together.

T12 — Why Founders Get the Compliance Conversation Wrong · December · Jake · Deep Domain Expertise (finance)

Audience: Founders and CTOs at high-growth finance companies treating compliance as a checklist to satisfy. What it is: R3 companion. The founder mistake: thinking compliance is something you satisfy after building, not an architectural constraint to design around. Why finance products that win at scale treat compliance like type safety — built in, automatic, invisible. Role: R3 launch companion. Drives Finance report downloads.

Email nurture flows (2)

Until now the content engine has treated downloadable submits as fuel for HubSpot lists and ad retargeting — but the email inboxes themselves haven't been worked. Email nurture closes that gap. Two flows because website downloads and LinkedIn Lead Gen submits come from very different intent levels.

Operating model. Both flows are short, link-heavy emails (3–5 sentences + a link), not long-form newsletters. The job is to pull the lead back to oakslab.com, where the existing content engine — industry pages, services pages, Situations pages, case studies, articles, KH library, and one-pagers — does the conversion work. Each email points at one specific asset. Built and run as HubSpot workflows.

Flow A — Website downloads (warm, came on purpose)

Trigger: Form submit on any gated downloadable from oakslab.com (D1–D4, plus R1/R2/R3 reports as they ship). Audience signal: Came to the site on purpose. Filled in a form deliberately. Likely already evaluating partners or product-development approaches. Cadence: 5 emails over ~4 weeks.
