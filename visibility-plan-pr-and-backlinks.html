01

Why this exists

We're producing original content based on research for the remainder of 2026, the kind of content journalists could actually cite. What's missing isn't more content on the roadmap, it's making sure the right people see what we've already planned.

What PR actually does for us. PR technically isn't a direct lead channel. Coverage and earned backlinks do two quieter jobs: they lift search and AI-search visibility for everything on oakslab.com, and they build the public credibility that gets us onto a CTO's shortlist, and into a VC's recommendation.

Four choices shape the plan:

  • We run PR ourselves. Relationships stay ours and the learning stays in-house. An agency can come in as a one-off for the August report if it clearly adds reach, but it may not be needed: the default is doing it ourselves.
  • One story, told broadly. The AI-Native angle goes to broad tech outlets where it has real pull. No vertical trade press.
  • Links earned, not bought. Report citations, podcast appearances, published quotes. No paid schemes, no guest-post farms.
  • Czech press goes first. The home market responds fastest to our story. The first clips and first relationships come from there, then carry into the international push.

06

Earned backlinks

Its own workstream, not a byproduct. Backlinks tend not to build up on their own, so this gets a light, recurring rhythm of its own.

  • Daily (from June or July, once signed up): about ten minutes a day on the journalist-request platforms (HARO, Qwoted, Connectively, Help a B2B Writer), answering only the requests that fit AI delivery, engineering leadership, or scaling. Every published quote is a link and a journalist who now knows us.
  • Monthly: one proactive action. Either a contributed article on a publication that accepts them and reaches engineering leaders, the strongest fits are The New Stack, InfoQ, and DZone, with developer communities like DEV.to and Hashnode as easier, lower-effort wins, or getting OAK'S LAB added to a credible "best engineering partners" roundup.
  • Podcasts (opportunistic): when one of Jake's posts clears 3% engagement, that topic is podcast-ready, two to three pitches a quarter. Each appearance brings a link from the host's site.
  • Per report launch: chase citations from the press push so the coverage turns into links.
  • Ongoing: log every earned link in a simple tracker, reviewed at the December audit against the domain-authority baseline.

On the newswire: recommended pick is EIN Presswire (~$149 a release, so both come to ~7k CZK, under budget). Premium wires (PR Newswire, Business Wire) run $1,000–3,000+ and are overkill for amplification.

08

Ideal outcomes

What good looks like by December. The year-end audit measures the plan against these and decides what gets doubled down on or cut for 2027.

  • Coverage: at least one Czech outlet has run the founder story, and the August report earned international pickup or citations.
  • Relationships: a working list of journalists who know OAK'S LAB and have replied at least once. That's the asset that makes 2027 pitching warm instead of cold.
  • Authority: measurable backlink growth against the domain-authority baseline, all earned: report citations, podcast appearances, published quotes.
  • Presence: all six directories claimed and current, so every "best engineering partner" search path finds an up-to-date profile.
  • A repeatable playbook: we'll know which track produced results per crown spent, and 2027 starts with a tested system instead of a hypothesis.
